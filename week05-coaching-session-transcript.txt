That's a reasonable intuition, and you're on the right track — but let me

Salt rounds actually control the cost factor of the bcrypt algorithm. Each

Here's a hint to think about: bcrypt.hash() is intentionally designed to be

--- COACH ---
You're partially right — the slowness is there either way. But the key

Node.js runs on a single thread. If bcrypt.hash() were synchronous and blocking,

That's a really important concept for Node.js applications — non-blocking I/O
is what keeps them performant under load.

That's a solid explanation. And I notice you're checking both !req.session AND

Good instinct to question it — it looks the same at first glance, but there's a

By checking !req.session first, you're protecting against that crash. It's a
small defensive detail, but it makes your middleware more robust.
